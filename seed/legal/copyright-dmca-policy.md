BOOSIE NETWORK COPYRIGHT & DMCA POLICY
Effective Date: August 29, 2026
BOOSIE NETWORK | LEGAL & POLICY
LEGAL REVIEW NOTE: Comprehensive operational draft. Have qualified counsel review and approve before public launch.
1. Respect for Rights
Boosie Network respects intellectual property rights and expects users, artists, creators, advertisers, and partners to do the same.
2. Copyright Complaints
A copyright owner or authorized agent who believes material on the Services infringes copyright may submit a notice identifying the copyrighted work, the allegedly infringing material and its location, contact information, a good-faith statement, an accuracy/authority statement under penalty of perjury, and a physical or electronic signature, consistent with 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3).
3. Designated Agent
Before launch, Boosie Network should register and publish its DMCA Designated Agent with the U.S. Copyright Office. Notices should be directed to the published designated-agent contact. Until that registration is completed, legal@boosienetwork.com may be used for intake but should not be represented as a registered agent unless it actually is.
4. Counter-Notification
A user whose material was removed may submit a legally compliant counter-notification identifying the removed material, stating under penalty of perjury a good-faith belief that removal resulted from mistake or misidentification, providing required contact and jurisdictional consent information, and signing the notice. Boosie Network may restore material as permitted by law if the claimant does not timely seek a court order.
5. Repeat Infringers
In appropriate circumstances, Boosie Network may terminate accounts of repeat infringers and may restrict users who repeatedly submit unauthorized content.
6. Music and Samples
Uploading music requires rights to the master recording, composition, samples, beats, features, artwork, and other protected elements as applicable. A subscription, artist account, or radio add-on does not clear rights.
7. Misrepresentations
Knowingly materially misrepresenting infringement or mistake may create liability under applicable law. Parties should submit claims in good faith.
